# Practice narration — the English script and how it is read

Written by `build.py`; do not edit by hand.

Every practice is read at about 95 words a minute, which is much slower than ordinary
narration. `hold` is the silence, in seconds, that follows a cue; it is added by the app,
not spoken. The seconds in brackets are how long the English recording of the cue runs.

## The six voices

### Maren — Primary — most seated meditation (10 practices)

- **Who:** Female voice in her 30s. Low pitch, warm timbre, very slow meditative pacing.
- **Timbre:** Warm and rounded, no edge. Chest resonance rather than head.
- **Tone:** Unhurried, kind, entirely unperformed. She is not selling anything.
- **Power:** Soft-to-medium. Speaking to one person a metre away, not to a room.
- **Emotion:** Settled. Warmth without sweetness — the register of someone who has done this a long time and is not impressed by it.
- **Expression:** Almost none. Meaning carried by pace and pause rather than inflection. Statements fall at the end; no upward lilt.
- **Never:** Any brightness or lift. If she sounds encouraging, she is wrong.

### Juniper — Morning, rousing, kapha-reducing (12 practices)

- **Who:** Female voice in her late 20s. Normal pitch, bright clear timbre, slow unhurried pacing.
- **Timbre:** Clear and forward, a little air in it. Brighter than Maren without being thin.
- **Tone:** Awake and level. Morning without cheerfulness.
- **Power:** Medium. Present, carrying, but never projecting.
- **Emotion:** Quiet readiness. The tone of opening a curtain, not of starting a class.
- **Expression:** Slightly more inflection than Maren — enough to keep a 6 a.m. listener with her. Still no enthusiasm.
- **Never:** Perkiness. This is the voice most at risk of sounding like a fitness app.

### Rowan — Grounding, vata-pacifying, earth visualisations (10 practices)

- **Who:** Male voice in his 40s. Low pitch, warm gravelly timbre, very slow meditative pacing.
- **Timbre:** Low and slightly gravelly. Weight in it. The audible equivalent of the practices it carries.
- **Tone:** Steady to the point of being immovable. Nothing hurried, nothing raised.
- **Power:** Soft but dense. Quiet without being breathy.
- **Emotion:** Calm that does not need to reassure. Presence rather than comfort.
- **Expression:** Minimal. Long falling lines. Comfortable leaving a sentence to sit.
- **Never:** Gravitas as performance. He is not narrating a documentary.

### Solene — Sleep, yoga nidra, night (8 practices)

- **Who:** Female voice in her 40s. Low pitch, soft breathy timbre, extremely slow hushed pacing.
- **Timbre:** Very soft, breath audible in it, close-miked. Almost but not quite a whisper.
- **Tone:** Descending throughout. Every line lower and slower than the one before.
- **Power:** Lowest in the roster. Should be barely above the ambience.
- **Emotion:** Nothing being asked. This voice is designed to be abandoned partway through.
- **Expression:** Flat by design. No emphasis anywhere — emphasis wakes people.
- **Never:** Any rise in pitch or pace, ever. A single lift undoes twenty minutes.

### Ash — Instructional — pranayama, contraindications, eating (12 practices)

- **Who:** Male voice in his 30s. Normal pitch, clear even timbre, slow measured pacing.
- **Timbre:** Clean and even. The least coloured voice in the roster, deliberately.
- **Tone:** Plain and precise. A competent teacher explaining a technique.
- **Power:** Medium and consistent. Intelligibility is the priority here, not intimacy.
- **Emotion:** Neutral. These practices have real contraindications and a soothing voice reading a safety warning is worse than useless.
- **Expression:** Ordinary speech rhythm, slowed. Numbers and counts land clearly.
- **Never:** Warmth. Warmth over an instruction makes it sound optional.

### Vera — Inquiry, impermanence, equanimity (8 practices)

- **Who:** Female voice in her 50s. Low pitch, dry smooth timbre, slow spacious pacing.
- **Timbre:** Dry, smooth, no vibrato. Age audible and unhidden.
- **Tone:** Unsentimental. Says difficult things without softening them.
- **Power:** Soft-to-medium, very even. Never leans in.
- **Emotion:** Level regard. These practices look directly at death, envy and helplessness; a comforting voice would be lying about them.
- **Expression:** Long pauses after the hard lines, and no reassurance afterwards.
- **Never:** Consolation. The silence after the sentence is doing the work.

## The practices

### So Hum (`so-hum-breath`) — meditation, read by Maren

00. Sit so that the weight of your upper body drops straight down into the floor. Let the hands rest where they fall. _(hold 8 s; English 13.6 s)_
01. Do nothing to the breath. Let it be as short or as shallow as it wants to be for now. _(hold 15 s; English 11.8 s)_
02. Notice that the in-breath makes a faint sound at the back of the throat. Something like so. _(hold 12 s; English 6.8 s)_
03. And the out-breath makes a different one. Something like hum. _(hold 12 s; English 8.6 s)_
04. So on the way in. Hum on the way out. You are not saying it. You are noticing that it is already being said. _(hold 20 s; English 16.6 s)_
05. If the breath is too quiet to hear, imagine the sound instead. It works the same way. _(hold 18 s; English 11.8 s)_
06. So. Hum. Nothing else to do with it. _(hold 40 s; English 6.2 s)_
07. When you notice you have gone somewhere else — and you will — that noticing is not a failure. It is the practice. Come back to so. _(hold 30 s; English 12.5 s)_
08. So on the way in. Hum on the way out. _(hold 60 s; English 7.0 s)_
09. Let the mantra go. Sit with whatever is left. _(hold 45 s; English 9.6 s)_
10. Let the breath come back under its own management. Feel the floor again. _(hold 15 s; English 8.4 s)_

### Counting the Exhale (`counting-the-exhale`) — meditation, read by Ash

00. Sit upright but not stiff. The spine long, the shoulders heavy. _(hold 10 s; English 6.9 s)_
01. Breathe in without counting. Then, as the breath leaves, count one. _(hold 12 s; English 8.1 s)_
02. In again, no count. Out — two. _(hold 14 s; English 6.3 s)_
03. Only the out-breath carries a number. The in-breath is free. _(hold 16 s; English 5.6 s)_
04. Go up to ten, then begin again at one. _(hold 45 s; English 3.9 s)_
05. If you find yourself at fifteen, the mind has gone for a walk. No matter. Start again at one. _(hold 40 s; English 9.0 s)_
06. If you lose the number entirely, start again at one. Beginning again is not starting over. _(hold 60 s; English 8.9 s)_
07. Keep counting. One to ten, then one again. _(hold 90 s; English 8.5 s)_
08. Now let the counting fall away and just breathe. _(hold 40 s; English 5.0 s)_
09. Notice whether the breath is different from when you began. It usually is. _(hold 20 s; English 6.0 s)_

### The Pause Between Breaths (`the-pause-between-breaths`) — meditation, read by Maren

00. Sit where you will not need to move for a while. _(hold 10 s; English 5.7 s)_
01. Let the breath find its own rhythm. This practice never asks you to change it. _(hold 20 s; English 14.3 s)_
02. Follow one breath all the way out. Notice that at the very end, before the next one starts, there is a pause. _(hold 18 s; English 15.9 s)_
03. It might be half a second. It does not matter how long it is. Only that you are awake inside it. _(hold 20 s; English 9.5 s)_
04. Rest your attention there. At the bottom of the breath, in the turn. _(hold 45 s; English 7.3 s)_
05. There is a second gap, at the top, after the in-breath completes. Find that one too. _(hold 30 s; English 9.0 s)_
06. Two still points in every breath. Nothing is being held. You are only noticing what was always there. _(hold 60 s; English 16.2 s)_
07. If the breath starts to feel effortful, you are interfering. Let it go entirely for three breaths, then return. _(hold 40 s; English 11.0 s)_
08. Stay with the turn of the breath. _(hold 90 s; English 3.9 s)_
09. Let the attention widen again. Feel the whole body breathing. _(hold 25 s; English 8.4 s)_

### Cooling Attention (`cooling-attention`) — meditation, read by Maren

00. Sit somewhere you are not too warm. Loosen anything tight at the neck or waist. _(hold 12 s; English 10.8 s)_
01. Take one slow breath in through the nose and notice something specific: the air is cooler going in than coming out. _(hold 18 s; English 18.0 s)_
02. Find that coolness at the rim of the nostrils. It is faint. Look for it anyway. _(hold 20 s; English 12.6 s)_
03. Every in-breath brings it back. Stay at the nostrils and let the coolness arrive again and again. _(hold 45 s; English 11.0 s)_
04. Now widen a little. Where else is the body cool? The palms, perhaps. The skin behind the ears. _(hold 25 s; English 12.4 s)_
05. Do not go looking for heat. Only for the cool places, and rest there. _(hold 40 s; English 8.6 s)_
06. If a hot thought arrives — something someone said, something unfinished — notice where it lands in the body, and then return to the coolest place you can find. _(hold 35 s; English 20.1 s)_
07. Cool air at the nostrils. Cool skin at the palms. Nothing to solve. _(hold 75 s; English 10.3 s)_
08. Let the whole face soften. The jaw, the space between the eyebrows. _(hold 30 s; English 8.7 s)_
09. Come back slowly. Heat does not leave in a hurry, and it does not need to. _(hold 20 s; English 10.0 s)_

### Weight and Contact (`weight-and-contact`) — meditation, read by Rowan

00. Sit or lie down. Whichever lets you be heaviest. _(hold 10 s; English 6.2 s)_
01. Find where the body touches the floor. The sitting bones, the backs of the legs, the heels. _(hold 18 s; English 10.1 s)_
02. Do not describe it. Just feel it. The pressure, the temperature, the exact edge where you stop and the floor begins. _(hold 22 s; English 18.4 s)_
03. Now let weight go into those places. As if you were slightly heavier than you thought. _(hold 25 s; English 7.0 s)_
04. The floor is holding all of it. It has been the whole time. _(hold 40 s; English 4.4 s)_
05. Move to the hands. Wherever they rest, feel the contact — the weight of them, the warmth underneath. _(hold 30 s; English 14.3 s)_
06. Now the whole underside of the body at once. Every place that is being supported. _(hold 60 s; English 14.8 s)_
07. If the mind lifts off into planning, it will. Come back to weight. Weight is always here and it is never interesting, which is exactly what makes it useful. _(hold 45 s; English 17.0 s)_
08. Heavy, supported, still. _(hold 90 s; English 4.9 s)_
09. Before you move, notice the difference between the body you sat down with and this one. _(hold 20 s; English 8.3 s)_

### Rotation of Awareness (`rotation-of-awareness`) — meditation, read by Rowan

00. Lie on your back, arms a little away from the sides, palms up. _(hold 12 s; English 6.6 s)_
01. We will move through the body in a fixed order. You do not have to remember it. Just follow. _(hold 12 s; English 8.4 s)_
02. Right hand thumb. Second finger. Third. Fourth. Fifth. The palm. The back of the hand. The wrist. _(hold 8 s; English 17.4 s)_
03. The forearm. The elbow. The upper arm. The shoulder. The armpit. The right side of the waist. The right hip. _(hold 8 s; English 11.6 s)_
04. The right thigh. The knee. The calf. The ankle. The heel. The sole of the right foot. The right big toe. _(hold 10 s; English 14.8 s)_
05. Now the same on the left. Left hand thumb. Second finger. Third. Fourth. Fifth. The palm. The wrist. _(hold 8 s; English 14.6 s)_
06. The forearm. The elbow. The upper arm. The shoulder. The left side of the waist. The left hip. _(hold 8 s; English 17.3 s)_
07. The left thigh. The knee. The calf. The ankle. The heel. The sole of the left foot. The left big toe. _(hold 10 s; English 18.9 s)_
08. Now the back of the body. The right shoulder blade. The left. The whole spine, from the base to the neck. _(hold 12 s; English 11.9 s)_
09. The back of the head. The crown. The forehead. The right eyebrow. The left. The space between them. _(hold 12 s; English 13.0 s)_
10. The right eyelid. The left. The right cheek. The left. The upper lip. The lower lip. The chin. The throat. _(hold 12 s; English 23.0 s)_
11. The chest. The stomach. The navel. The whole front of the body at once. _(hold 20 s; English 10.4 s)_
12. Now the whole body at once. Every part you just visited, all together. _(hold 60 s; English 9.0 s)_
13. Stay here. Nothing to do. _(hold 90 s; English 3.9 s)_
14. When you are ready, let one hand move. Then the other. Take your time coming back. _(hold 25 s; English 12.0 s)_

### Silent Repetition (`silent-mantra`) — meditation, read by Rowan

00. Sit with the spine upright and the hands still. _(hold 12 s; English 6.6 s)_
01. Choose one word. Om, if nothing else comes. Or peace. Or a name that means something to you. _(hold 20 s; English 12.1 s)_
02. Say it once out loud, so the body knows the shape of it. _(hold 15 s; English 5.5 s)_
03. Now say it under the breath, barely moving the lips. Ten times, at whatever pace feels natural. _(hold 40 s; English 15.4 s)_
04. Now stop moving the lips altogether. Say it only inwardly. _(hold 20 s; English 9.7 s)_
05. Let it settle onto the breath. One repetition as the breath goes out, or one on each breath. Let it find its own place. _(hold 60 s; English 12.6 s)_
06. The word will change texture. It will get faster, or lose its meaning, or seem to say itself. All of that is fine. _(hold 45 s; English 12.1 s)_
07. When you lose it, pick it up again without comment. _(hold 90 s; English 5.0 s)_
08. Now let the word stop. Do not replace it with anything. _(hold 60 s; English 9.0 s)_
09. Sit in what is left of it. _(hold 45 s; English 3.2 s)_

### The Sound and Its Ending (`om-resonance`) — meditation, read by Juniper

00. Sit upright. This one is loud, so choose your moment. _(hold 12 s; English 5.5 s)_
01. Take a full breath in, and on the way out let a long low sound come from the chest. Aaaa. _(hold 16 s; English 11.3 s)_
02. Again. Feel where it vibrates — low in the body, behind the breastbone. _(hold 20 s; English 6.9 s)_
03. Now the second sound, higher, in the throat and mouth. Ooooo. _(hold 18 s; English 8.2 s)_
04. And the third, with the lips closed, in the nose and the skull. Mmmmm. _(hold 18 s; English 6.7 s)_
05. Now all three, joined, on one breath. A into O into M. _(hold 25 s; English 10.3 s)_
06. Again. Let it be long. Let the M go on until the breath is gone. _(hold 30 s; English 7.9 s)_
07. Three more, at your own pace. Notice that the sound gets quieter each time without being told to. _(hold 60 s; English 8.3 s)_
08. Now stop. Do not chant. Listen to what the sound left behind. _(hold 60 s; English 9.0 s)_
09. That silence is the fourth part. It is the only part the text says is the real one. _(hold 45 s; English 6.9 s)_
10. Come back when the silence has thinned out. _(hold 20 s; English 4.0 s)_

### The Fading Bell (`the-fading-bell`) — meditation, read by Maren

00. Sit still. If you have a bowl or a bell, have it within reach. If not, the recording will do. _(hold 15 s; English 14.8 s)_
01. Strike it once, softly. _(hold 6 s; English 4.4 s)_
02. Follow the sound. Not the strike — what comes after it. _(hold 20 s; English 10.9 s)_
03. It is getting quieter. Stay with it as it goes. _(hold 25 s; English 9.6 s)_
04. At some point you will not be able to say whether you are hearing it or remembering it. Stay there. That place is the practice. _(hold 30 s; English 12.9 s)_
05. When it is entirely gone, wait in the silence. Do not strike again yet. _(hold 40 s; English 11.9 s)_
06. Strike it again. Follow it down. _(hold 60 s; English 4.8 s)_
07. Again, if you like. Each time, the ear gets finer. _(hold 60 s; English 6.8 s)_
08. Now stop striking. Listen to the room instead — whatever is there. Follow the quietest thing you can find. _(hold 60 s; English 11.0 s)_
09. Let the listening go. _(hold 30 s; English 8.8 s)_

### Watching Thought Arrive (`witness-of-thought`) — meditation, read by Vera

00. Sit comfortably. This one asks for a little patience. _(hold 12 s; English 5.2 s)_
01. Do not try to have a quiet mind. Let it do what it does. _(hold 25 s; English 6.7 s)_
02. A thought will arrive. When it does, notice the arriving rather than the content. _(hold 30 s; English 8.0 s)_
03. Where did it come from? You did not decide to have it. It appeared. _(hold 25 s; English 6.6 s)_
04. Watch the next one appear the same way. Not what it says. That it came. _(hold 45 s; English 6.1 s)_
05. Now notice something else: whatever is doing the watching did not arrive. It was already here. _(hold 35 s; English 7.9 s)_
06. Rest as the thing that was already here. Let the thoughts pass in front of it like weather. _(hold 75 s; English 8.7 s)_
07. If you get pulled into a thought and forget — and you will — the moment you notice, you are back. There is no penalty. _(hold 40 s; English 10.7 s)_
08. Weather in front. Sky behind. Stay as the sky. _(hold 90 s; English 6.1 s)_
09. Let all of it go, including the image of the sky. _(hold 45 s; English 4.4 s)_

### Wishing Well (`loving-kindness`) — meditation, read by Maren

00. Sit however you can be comfortable for ten minutes. _(hold 12 s; English 6.2 s)_
01. Bring to mind the phrase: may I be safe. Say it inwardly and see whether anything answers. _(hold 25 s; English 11.7 s)_
02. May I be well. May I be at ease. May I live with some kindness toward myself. _(hold 35 s; English 12.1 s)_
03. If that feels false, it often does at first. Say it anyway. It is a direction, not a claim. _(hold 30 s; English 12.4 s)_
04. Now bring to mind someone easy. Someone you are simply glad exists. _(hold 20 s; English 10.2 s)_
05. May you be safe. May you be well. May you be at ease. _(hold 40 s; English 8.6 s)_
06. Now someone neutral. Someone you see and do not think about — a neighbour, someone behind a counter. _(hold 25 s; English 18.0 s)_
07. May you be safe. May you be well. May you be at ease. They have a whole life you know nothing about. _(hold 40 s; English 13.0 s)_
08. Now, only if you are willing, someone difficult. Not the hardest person. Start further down the list. _(hold 30 s; English 15.5 s)_
09. May you be safe. May you be well. You are not excusing anything. You are declining to carry it. _(hold 45 s; English 13.5 s)_
10. Now let it go outward with no particular object. Whoever is nearby. Whoever is awake right now. _(hold 45 s; English 13.3 s)_
11. Come back to yourself, and end where you began. May I be well. _(hold 30 s; English 11.1 s)_

### The Fire at the Navel (`fire-at-the-navel`) — meditation, read by Juniper

00. Sit upright, with the belly free — nothing tight at the waist. _(hold 15 s; English 4.8 s)_
01. Rest one hand two fingers below the navel. Feel the warmth of the hand through the cloth. _(hold 20 s; English 7.4 s)_
02. Breathe normally and notice that the belly moves under your hand. Do not push it. _(hold 25 s; English 6.3 s)_
03. Now find the warmth underneath. Not the hand's warmth. The body's. _(hold 30 s; English 6.3 s)_
04. It is faint. It is always there. This is the fire the texts call agni, and everything you eat passes through it. _(hold 25 s; English 12.5 s)_
05. On each in-breath, imagine air reaching it. On each out-breath, imagine it glowing a little steadier. _(hold 45 s; English 14.5 s)_
06. Not bigger. Steadier. A fire that is too big digests the wrong things. _(hold 40 s; English 7.6 s)_
07. Stay with the warmth below the navel. Breath feeding it, quietly. _(hold 75 s; English 6.7 s)_
08. Now take the hand away and see whether you can still find the warmth without it. _(hold 40 s; English 5.3 s)_
09. Let it be. It will keep going without your attention, as it has all your life. _(hold 25 s; English 6.7 s)_

### The Forest Floor (`the-forest-floor`) — visualisation, read by Rowan

00. Settle, and let the eyes close. _(hold 12 s; English 8.2 s)_
01. Put yourself on the floor of an old forest. Not a path through it — the floor itself. _(hold 20 s; English 12.6 s)_
02. Under you, a deep layer of leaves that has been building for longer than you have been alive. _(hold 25 s; English 8.0 s)_
03. Below that, roots. Not one tree's roots but every tree's, crossing and holding each other in the dark. _(hold 30 s; English 21.8 s)_
04. Nothing here is in a hurry. The tree above you has been at it for two hundred years and will be here after. _(hold 35 s; English 8.9 s)_
05. Smell the ground. Cold, mineral, faintly sweet. _(hold 30 s; English 9.7 s)_
06. Feel the weight of your own body pressing into the leaf litter, and the ground pressing evenly back. _(hold 45 s; English 9.3 s)_
07. Somewhere above, the canopy is moving. Down here it is entirely still. _(hold 60 s; English 13.4 s)_
08. Let yourself be one more heavy thing on the forest floor. Nothing is required of you. _(hold 90 s; English 11.5 s)_
09. The forest carries on. Let it. Come back slowly. _(hold 25 s; English 9.0 s)_

### The Still Lake (`the-still-lake`) — visualisation, read by Maren

00. Let the eyes close and the shoulders drop. _(hold 12 s; English 6.6 s)_
01. A lake, late in the day. The light has gone flat and orange. There is no wind at all. _(hold 25 s; English 13.0 s)_
02. The surface is completely unbroken. It is holding the whole sky upside down without effort. _(hold 30 s; English 13.5 s)_
03. Notice that the water is not doing anything to be still. It has simply stopped being disturbed. _(hold 35 s; English 11.6 s)_
04. Something touches the surface — an insect, a leaf. Rings go out. _(hold 25 s; English 9.0 s)_
05. Watch them travel, get wider, get fainter. Watch the surface close over again. _(hold 40 s; English 9.6 s)_
06. This is the whole teaching. The lake does not mind being disturbed. It just returns. _(hold 40 s; English 14.8 s)_
07. Let a thought be a leaf landing. Rings out, rings fading, surface closing. _(hold 75 s; English 14.4 s)_
08. The light is going. The water is getting darker and even more still. _(hold 60 s; English 7.4 s)_
09. Leave the lake there. It will be like that all night. _(hold 25 s; English 8.9 s)_

### The Hearth (`the-hearth`) — visualisation, read by Juniper

00. Sit, and let the eyes close. _(hold 12 s; English 2.8 s)_
01. A small fire in a grate. A cold room otherwise, and one warm side of you. _(hold 22 s; English 7.6 s)_
02. The heat is only on the front of the body. Your back is still cool. Feel both at once. _(hold 28 s; English 8.0 s)_
03. Watch what the fire actually does. It is never the same shape twice, and it never stops. _(hold 35 s; English 8.2 s)_
04. A log settles. A shower of sparks goes up and disappears. _(hold 30 s; English 7.0 s)_
05. There is no effort in it. Nothing is being achieved. It is simply burning because that is what it is. _(hold 40 s; English 7.5 s)_
06. Let the warmth on your face be the only thing you are attending to. _(hold 60 s; English 4.5 s)_
07. If you are tired or low, let the fire be doing the work for a while. You do not have to. _(hold 60 s; English 7.2 s)_
08. The fire is getting lower now, and steadier. Less flame, more heat. _(hold 45 s; English 6.8 s)_
09. Leave it burning down. Come back to the room. _(hold 25 s; English 5.8 s)_

### Wind Through Grass (`wind-through-grass`) — visualisation, read by Juniper

02. The wind is steady and not cold. It arrives in waves. _(hold 25 s; English 6.2 s)_

### The Night Sky (`the-night-sky`) — visualisation, read by Solene

00. Lie on your back if you can. Let the arms fall wherever they want. _(hold 15 s; English 8.9 s)_
01. You are outside, somewhere with no lights, on your back on the ground. _(hold 22 s; English 8.8 s)_
02. Above you the sky is completely open. No cloud, no moon. Just depth. _(hold 28 s; English 9.3 s)_
03. Let the eyes adjust in the way they do. More and more of it appears the longer you look. _(hold 35 s; English 9.0 s)_

06. Whatever you were worrying about is still true. It is just a different size from up here. _(hold 45 s; English 8.1 s)_
07. Stay with the openness. There is nothing to reach and nowhere to get to. _(hold 90 s; English 17.5 s)_
08. Feel the ground at your back again — the one solid thing in all of it. _(hold 40 s; English 11.5 s)_

### Complete Rest (`complete-rest`) — relaxation, read by Solene

00. Lie down on your back. Feet apart, arms away from the body, palms up. _(hold 15 s; English 8.1 s)_
01. Take one breath in through the nose, and let the next one out through the mouth with a sound. _(hold 15 s; English 10.8 s)_
02. Two more like that. Each one, let a little more of your weight go into the floor. _(hold 25 s; English 9.6 s)_
03. Now let the breath go back to normal and stop managing it. _(hold 25 s; English 8.6 s)_
04. Squeeze the right fist tight. Tighter. Hold it. Now let it go completely. _(hold 20 s; English 10.0 s)_
05. Notice the difference between the two hands. That difference is what we are after everywhere else. _(hold 25 s; English 9.3 s)_
06. Squeeze the left fist. Hold. Release. _(hold 20 s; English 5.7 s)_
07. Now the shoulders — lift them toward the ears. Hold. Drop them. _(hold 20 s; English 9.3 s)_
08. Screw up the whole face. Hold it. Let it fall apart. _(hold 20 s; English 7.6 s)_
09. Press the backs of the legs into the floor. Hold. Release. _(hold 20 s; English 8.3 s)_
10. Now nothing. No part of the body has a job. _(hold 60 s; English 6.7 s)_
11. If a thought arrives, let it. You are not meditating. You are resting. _(hold 90 s; English 8.8 s)_
12. Let the body be as heavy as it wants to be. _(hold 120 s; English 4.9 s)_
13. Start with the fingers. Then the toes. Take a bigger breath. Come back without hurrying. _(hold 30 s; English 17.5 s)_

### Yogic Sleep, Short Form (`yoga-nidra-short`) — relaxation, read by Solene

00. Lie on your back and get comfortable enough that you will not need to move. _(hold 20 s; English 8.3 s)_
01. The rule of this practice is one thing only: try to stay awake. Not alert. Awake. _(hold 20 s; English 13.4 s)_
02. Bring to mind one short intention. Something plainly true that you would like more of. Say it inwardly three times. _(hold 40 s; English 19.2 s)_
