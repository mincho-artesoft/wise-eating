## 1. Why this exists

MP-6b's 21-meal verbatim sample was generated by the real solver over 13,993 candidates and passed **23 of 23 hard properties**. It also contained this:

> **Day 2 · Breakfast** — Cilantro Coriander Herb Salt, *Infant formula, organic, powder*, *Infant formula, store brand, added rice*, Spices fenugreek seed, Mung Dal with Okra, Mung beans mature seeds raw
>
> **Day 3 · Dinner** — Nigella seed, *Toddler formula, store brand, pediatric shake*, Marjoram dried, Spices marjoram dried, Carom Seed, Black pepper
>
> **Day 1 · Breakfast** — Frozen yogurt chocolate, Chocolate milk whole, Milk shake with malt, Soy milk chocolate
>
> **Day 5 · Dinner** — Curry Leaves, Tulsi dried, Black Cumin, Lime leaf dried, Lentils pink or red raw, Nigella Seed

It served infant formula to an adult three times in one week. Every property passed because none of them asked whether the output was food. That is a director defect, not an implementation defect — the acceptance suite was mine.

Three causes, all fixable:

1. **`minAgeMonths` is a floor, not a range.** Infant formula carries a floor of 0 and therefore satisfies every adult age check in the system. The data can say "not before this age"; it cannot say "intended for infants, inappropriate for adults."
2. **Nothing in the catalogue records what a row is *for*.** A dried herb and a lentil dish are interchangeable to the solver.
3. **The tier preference actively rewards spices.** Classical-tier dravyas *are* mostly spices and herbs, so a solver told to prefer classical tier learns that eating a spice rack pacifies dosha. Y1's +0.5209 delta may be partly this artifact. See §6.

## 3. Sub-tasks

### MP-7a — FoodRoleResolver

Implement `food-roles.json` exactly as specified: evaluate every rule, discard any whose vetoes fire, take highest `priority`, break ties by longest matched phrase in tokens, then by file order. One role per row — roles are **not** a set and do **not** roll up a hierarchy, unlike `FoodConcepts`.

**Reuse the `FoodConcepts` matcher. Do not write a second one.** Same normalization, same contiguous token-sequence match, same order-independent plural-tolerant veto groups, token boundary only and never substring.

Cache the resolved role on `FoodItem` the same way concept sets are cached. Resolution must not run per-solve.

The three signal-driven rules were **all wrong in rev1 and are corrected in `food-roles.json` rev2**. The signal audit that caught them was correct and the stop was correct. What follows is the resolution; no further audit is needed.

| rev1 claimed | reality | rev2 does |
|---|---|---|
| `recipe.course` | **does not exist**. Real fields are `meal` (breakfast 155 / lunch 569 / dinner 367 / snack 163 / drink 133 / dessert 113) and `category` (classical 300 / everyday 563 / international 637) | split into two rules, below |
| `usda.foodCategory`, 25 SR Legacy values | field is `FoodItem.category`, an **array**; the first entry is the match, the convention `category-rules.json` already uses. Vocabulary is the finer WWEIA-style set — **187** values enumerated in `category-rules.json` | all **187** mapped |
| `dravya.classification`, Sanskrit vargas | field is `dravya.category`; **19 English values**, zero overlap with the Sanskrit keys | all **19** mapped |

On the recipe signal the audit's reasoning was exactly right: `meal` names a *slot*, not a role, and mapping breakfast/lunch/dinner/snack to a role would have been the prohibited proxy substitution. rev2 splits it:

- **`A-RECIPE-MEAL` (priority 85)** maps only `drink → beverage` and `dessert → sweet`. Those two are direct — a recipe whose meal is `drink` *is* a beverage. The other four values are not mapped here at all.
- **`A-RECIPE-COMPOSED` (priority 45)** is structural, not inferential: a row with **≥2 ingredients and ≥1 preparation step is a prepared dish**, therefore not a raw commodity and not a seasoning. It maps breakfast/lunch/dinner → `main`, snack → `side`. It sits **below** the 60-band name phrases, so it never overrides them — "Bathua Raita" still resolves to `side`, "Dry Fruit Ladoo" to `sweet`, "Cilantro Coriander Herb Salt" to `spice`. Only recipes whose names carry no signal reach it.

That rule is the weakest in the file and is gated separately at G2b.

Two gaps remain, as gates rather than blockers: roughly 53 of the ~240 live `FoodItem.category` values fall outside the 187 enumerated in `category-rules.json` (G1b), and the `regional` and `fermented` dravya categories are genuinely heterogeneous and mapped conservatively (see `weakEntries` in the file).

**Do not invent a proxy for anything still missing.** G12 rev1 used "not Vegan" as a stand-in for honey and produced 831 false disagreements out of 1,217. Report and stop instead — as you did.

### MP-7b — FC-1f, plural tolerance

`food-concepts.json` rev5 specifies plural tolerance as **trailing `s` only**. That rule does not match `potatoes` from `potato`, `tomatoes` from `tomato`, `berries` from `berry`, or `leaves` from `leaf`.

This was caught by running the new role goldens against the new role rules: *"Potatoes, flesh and skin, raw"* — the exact row behind the original FC-1 `flesh` defect — fell through to `other`.

Extend the matcher to: identical, `+s`, `+es`, `y→ies`, plus the `irregularPlurals` map in `food-roles.json`. Then **re-run all 117 exclusion goldens and the 53-case resolution holdout**. A broader matcher can create new false positives; if any golden flips, report it before proceeding.

### MP-7c — solver constraints

Hard, checked at candidate-filter time (cheapest place, and it shrinks the pool):

- role declares `eligibleAsComponent: false` → ineligible. `infantProduct` becomes eligible only when profile age < 36 months.
- `requiresCooking` → ineligible.

Hard, checked during meal assembly:

- ≥1 component with `anchor: true` per meal.
- per-role `maxPerMeal`; combined cap of 2 across `spice`+`herb`+`condiment`+`medicinalHerb`; `beverage` ≤ 2.
- portion grams clamped to the role's `portionGrams` range **before** the calorie repair pass, not after. The repair pass must not be able to push a spice to 80 g to hit a calorie target — that is exactly how the observed plans reached their exact 500/700/800 totals.

Soft:

- near-duplicate penalty using the `isNearDuplicateOf` headword test.
- role-balance objective: reward a meal shaped anchor + side + at most one seasoning.

The adaptive dish-count logic from MP-5 must be re-checked against the new clamps: narrowing per-role portion ranges narrows the reachable calorie span, and P7 (1200 kcal) / P8 (4000 kcal) are the properties that catch it.

### MP-7d — C1–C10 checker

Sample **60 rows uniformly at random from the real 14,484-row catalogue with a fixed, reported seed**. Hand-label them **before** running the resolver. Report that score separately. That is the number that counts.

**If G0 does not hold, stop and report.** A suite written from observed failures that cannot detect those failures is evidence of nothing, and every gate below it becomes meaningless.

**G1 — role coverage.** Over a 500-row stratified sample (proportional across classical 336 / derived 1,969 / estimated 10,296) plus all 91 distinct food IDs the MP-6b sample selected: rows resolving to `other` ≤ **10%**. Above that, stop and report — do not tighten the solver against a map full of holes.

**G1b — category map coverage.** Report every distinct `FoodItem.category[0]` value **not** present in the 187-entry map, with its row count. Unmapped values must account for ≤ **2%** of catalogue rows. Above that, report the list and stop — I will author the missing entries rather than have you guess them.

**G2 — holdout.** Of 60 hand-labelled random rows: wrong-confident (a specific role where the label says a different specific role) ≤ **3**; unresolved (`other`) ≤ **6**. **Any** wrong-confident assignment involving `infantProduct`, `supplement` or `nonFood` is an automatic stop, in either direction.

**G2b — the composed-dish fallback.** `A-RECIPE-COMPOSED` is the weakest rule in the file, so measure it on its own. Take the authored recipes that actually reach it — those whose names fired no 60-band rule — sample **40** at random with a reported seed, hand-label them, and report accuracy. Also report **how many of the 1,500 recipes reach it at all**; if the name rules already resolve most of them, the rule matters less than its weakness suggests. Below **75%** accuracy, report and stop rather than tuning it yourself.

**G3 — cross-derivation.** Derive every row's role twice: name rules with the category map disabled, category map with the name rules disabled. Enumerate all disagreements, report the count, and review a 100-row random sample of them by hand. **Do not auto-resolve.** This is the only technique that found the `flesh` phrase matching 46 vegetarian recipes; no single-source check would have. A large raw count is expected and is not itself a failure.

**G4 — no regressions.** 46 properties; 123 existing tests; 117 exclusion goldens; resolution holdout ≥ 44/48; integration goldens 25/25 + 2/2; MP-6 narration still byte-identical across 100 runs; total model calls still **2**.

**G5 — feasibility.** All 10 profiles × 3 horizons = 30 solves. ≥ **28** must produce a plan. Any infeasible solve must be *reported* naming the binding constraint (properties F1/F2). **Never relax a plausibility constraint to find a solution** — emitting a partial plan, or quietly dropping a cap, is the worst available behaviour.

**G6 — Y1 must survive.** Mean dosha-pacification delta across profiles ≥ **+0.30** (rev1 measured +0.5209, range 0.0933–0.7553). See §6 before treating a drop as a bug.

## 6. The risk worth naming up front

**Y1 may collapse, and that would be a finding rather than a failure.**

The tier preference rewards classical-tier dravyas, which are overwhelmingly spices and herbs. C3 caps seasonings at 2 per meal. If the +0.5209 pacification delta was substantially produced by the solver loading meals with classical-tier spices, capping them will shrink it.

If that happens, the honest conclusion is that a meaningful share of the measured Ayurvedic effect was an artifact of the spice bias rather than dosha-appropriate food selection — and the fix is to weight dosha effect by *portion actually consumed* rather than by presence, so 2 g of dried marjoram cannot pacify a constitution as much as 200 g of a suitable main dish. Do not paper over a drop by raising the C3 cap.

Report the delta before and after, per profile, and let the number stand.
